Methodology

What the numbers on this site mean, and their limits.

On "Champions League" — StatsBomb's free open data only includes the final of each Champions League season, not a full season of group and knockout matches. What's shown here is a pooled archive of 18 finals from 1970 through 2018/19, spanning many different clubs across different eras — not one full competition the way the Premier League dataset is.

The xG model

Each shot's expected-goals value comes from one logistic regression trained on shot distance and shot angle to the goal mouth, fit across every non-penalty shot in both datasets pooled together. StatsBomb's own statsbomb_xg field is also kept in the shot data for comparison — this model is intentionally simpler, since the goal is to demonstrate the concept end to end.

The pass network

Each club's network aggregates touch locations and completed passes across every match they played in the dataset, counting only play up to each match's first substitution. Only the 16 busiest players and their top 40 passing links are shown per club, to keep the diagram readable.

The value score

Player scores compare xG-per-90 (StatsBomb's open data doesn't carry precise minutes played, so every match a player took a shot in is treated as a full 90 minutes) against a market value figure, normalized 0–100 within the dataset. The market values in data/market_values.csv are illustrative sample figures for this demo, not live data — StatsBomb doesn't provide valuations, so this reads them from a plain CSV you can replace with real figures.
